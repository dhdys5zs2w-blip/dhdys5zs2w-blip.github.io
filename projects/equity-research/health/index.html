How to read this

A lamp is steady when a check reads as expected and pulses amber when it does not; grey means nothing to judge yet. “On schedule” is judged in the browser, by its own clock: tonight's bar is due once the 18:30 run has had until 19:20 to finish (the 22:30 retry until 23:20), and the lag is counted in weekdays — exchange holidays are not known to this page, so a holiday reads as one weekday behind.

These are symptoms the page can see, not a certificate: a run that wrote the wrong numbers on time passes every check here.

How to read this

Each bar runs along the last few weekdays and stops at the table's newest date. The solid line is the newest price bar; the dashed line is the date tonight's schedule says should be in. A bar is amber only when it trails prices by more than its table legitimately may — targets by one weekday, everything else by none. The prices row itself is measured against the schedule instead.

Indicators are held to none although the note above says they may lag: the nightly run computes them through the bar it has just fetched, so the table's newest date should match prices, and a day's gap there is a step worth checking rather than the lag the note allows.

Lags are counted in weekdays by the browser; exchange holidays are not known to this page.
