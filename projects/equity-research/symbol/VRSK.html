How these are computed

Descriptive, not predictive. SE is s/√n and assumes independent days; buckets mix regimes across the whole sample. The n is shown so you can judge how little a bucket mean can carry.

  • Monthly returns compound the daily adjusted-close returns inside each calendar month; a partial month is shown with its day count rather than hidden.
  • Drawdowns are the adjusted close against its running maximum; an episode still under water is marked ongoing.
  • Rolling volatility is the 63-day standard deviation of daily returns, annualized by √252.
  • Rolling beta is the stored 60-day beta_spy_v1 indicator, which exists only while the symbol is in a scored universe — line breaks are membership gaps.
  • The distribution shows every daily return with a normal curve of the same mean and standard deviation overlaid; skew and excess kurtosis are reported beside it. Fewer than 60 returns is not drawn.
  • Relative strength is the cumulative ratio of the stock to SPY, rebased to 1 at the first date both have a price — the base date is arbitrary.

Call−put spread and 25-delta skew

Implied-vol points at 30 days: call minus put at the money, and out-of-the-money put minus call.

How to read this

One chain per week, the last session of the week, reduced to three numbers. ATM implied vol is the market's price of the next 30 days of variance for this name; drawn against the trailing 20-day realized vol, the gap is the variance premium being paid. The call−put spread at the money and the 25-delta skew (out-of-the-money put vol minus call vol) are the two cross-sectional signals the literature reports, under a registered test here that has not run yet. Weekly sampling means a line break is a skipped week or a name without listed options, not missing data.
